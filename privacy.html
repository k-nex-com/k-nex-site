NexMove (desktop app)

NexMove copies your files from Dropbox to Google Drive, from Dropbox to OneDrive, or from OneDrive to Google Drive. It runs entirely on your own Windows computer. Your files travel from where they are now to your computer and on to where they are going; they never pass through, and are never stored on, K-NEX servers.

Signing in to Dropbox, OneDrive and Google

You sign in on Dropbox’s, Microsoft’s and Google’s own web pages, and only to the two services in the move you picked. K-NEX never sees your passwords. After you sign in, each service gives NexMove an access token, which is saved only on your computer, in NexMove’s settings folder. It is never sent to K-NEX.

  • Dropbox: NexMove reads your files and folders so it can copy them. It does not change, move or delete anything in your Dropbox.
  • Google Drive: NexMove asks only for access to files it creates itself (the drive.file permission). It creates a folder called “From Dropbox” or “From OneDrive”, copies your files into it and checks them afterwards. It cannot see or change any other files in your Google Drive.
  • OneDrive: Microsoft has no permission as narrow as Google’s, so NexMove asks for access to your OneDrive files. Right after you sign in, it asks Microsoft which OneDrive belongs to your account (the drive’s ID and type) so it uses the right one. When you copy from OneDrive, NexMove reads your files and folders and does not change, move or delete anything; it cannot open your Personal Vault and skips it. When you copy to OneDrive, it creates a folder called “From Dropbox”, copies your files into it and checks them, and does not change or delete your other files.

What stays on your computer

NexMove keeps a log of what it copied and any errors, and remembers which folders you chose, so it can carry on where it stopped. It also keeps your licence key and how much of the free test copy you have used. These stay on your computer. NexMove has no analytics or tracking and does not send usage data to K-NEX.

When something leaves your computer

If you have a licence, NexMove asks Dropbox for your account ID, or Microsoft for your OneDrive’s drive ID, and keeps a short one-way code made from it on your computer, so the licence stays with one account you copy from. When you enter a licence key, NexMove sends the key and a label with its version, operating system and that code to Lemon Squeezy. Each time you start copying, it sends the key again to check it still works. Nothing else about you or your files is sent.

Something is sent to K-NEX only when you press “Send report” or “Get help”. Before anything is sent, NexMove shows you exactly what will be sent. Sign-in tokens are always removed, you can choose to hide file and folder names, and the contents of your files are never sent. See the table above for what a report contains and how long we keep it.

Google user data

NexMove’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. NexMove uses Google Drive access only to copy your files into your own Google Drive and to check that they arrived. That data stays on your computer and in your Google Drive. It is not transferred to K-NEX or anyone else, not used for advertising, and not read by people.

Removing NexMove’s access

Uninstalling NexMove deletes the app, but you can also remove its access at any time:

  • Google: myaccount.google.com/permissions
  • Dropbox: dropbox.com/account/connected_apps
  • Microsoft (OneDrive): account.live.com/consent/Manage

Files NexMove copied stay in your Google Drive or OneDrive until you delete them.
