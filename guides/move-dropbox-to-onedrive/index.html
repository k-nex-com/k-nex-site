How to move your files from Dropbox to OneDrive

BY TIM KIM · UPDATED 31 OCTOBER 2026 · 6 MIN READ

Already paying for Microsoft 365, and wondering why you still pay for Dropbox too? Microsoft 365 comes with OneDrive storage, so moving your Dropbox files there can save you a subscription. There are three ways to do it. The right one depends on how much you have and how much time you want to spend checking it.

IN THIS GUIDE

  1. Before you start: check your OneDrive storage and file names
  2. Way 1: Download from Dropbox, upload to OneDrive
  3. Way 2: Use the Dropbox and OneDrive apps on your PC
  4. Way 3: Let an app copy and check it for you

Before you start: check your OneDrive storage and file names

A free Microsoft account comes with 5 GB, shared between OneDrive, Outlook.com attachments and Microsoft 365 apps. Microsoft 365 Basic gives you 100 GB and Microsoft 365 Personal 1 TB. Compare that with how much you use in Dropbox:

  1. In Dropbox, open your account settings and look at Plan to see how much space you use.
  2. Go to onedrive.live.com/?v=managestorage to see how much OneDrive space you have left.

If Dropbox uses more than you have free in OneDrive, get more storage first, or clear out what you no longer need. Running out halfway is the most common reason a move goes wrong.

OneDrive is also stricter about names than Dropbox. It does not allow the characters " * : < > ? / \ |, spaces at the start or end of a name, or names such as CON, PRN, AUX, NUL, desktop.ini or anything starting with ~$ (Microsoft Support). Files with names like these can’t go into OneDrive as they are, so rename them first if you have any. They are most common in files made on a Mac.

Way 1: Download from Dropbox, upload to OneDrive

Best for: a few folders, a few gigabytes. Free, nothing to install.

  1. Sign in at dropbox.com.
  2. Select a folder, then choose Download. Dropbox gives you a .zip file.
  3. On your computer, open the .zip and choose Extract all.
  4. Go to onedrive.live.com, choose Upload → Folder, and pick the extracted folder.
  5. Repeat for each folder.

Watch out: dropbox.com can only download a folder if it is smaller than 250 GB and holds fewer than 10,000 files, counting everything in its subfolders (Dropbox Help). Bigger folders have to be downloaded in parts, and it is up to you to keep track of which parts are done. Microsoft also suggests using the OneDrive app instead of the website for any file larger than a few gigabytes. Your computer needs enough free space to hold the download and the extracted copy at the same time.

Way 2: Use the Dropbox and OneDrive apps on your PC

Best for: people comfortable with File Explorer, with enough free disk space for all their files. Free.

  1. Install the Dropbox desktop app and sign in. OneDrive is already part of Windows 10 and 11: sign in to it with your Microsoft account.
  2. In File Explorer, make sure your Dropbox files are really on your PC. Files marked as online-only are just shortcuts: right-click the Dropbox folder and choose Make available offline, then wait for the download to finish.
  3. Open your OneDrive folder in File Explorer.
  4. Copy (don’t move) your Dropbox folders into the OneDrive folder. OneDrive uploads them in the background.
  5. Leave the PC on until OneDrive shows that everything is up to date.

Watch out: use Copy, not Cut. If you cut files out of the Dropbox folder, Dropbox deletes them from your account too. OneDrive lists any file it could not upload, for example because of its name, but among thousands of files it is easy not to notice.

Checklist before you cancel Dropbox

Whichever way you choose, go through this list before you stop paying:

  • Compare the totals. Check that the number of files and the total size in OneDrive roughly match Dropbox, folder by folder for the ones that matter most.
  • Open a few files. Pick some old photos and documents at random and make sure they open from OneDrive.
  • Folders shared with you. Folders other people shared with you belong to them. Copy anything you want to keep, because you may lose access later.
  • Links you shared. Links you sent to others point to Dropbox. Share the files again from OneDrive if people still need them.
  • Dropbox-only documents. Dropbox Paper documents may not open in OneDrive. Export them to Word or PDF first.
  • Phone photo backup. If the Dropbox app uploads your phone’s photos, turn on camera upload in the OneDrive app instead, so new photos don’t go missing.
  • Version history and deleted files. These are not copied. If you need an older version of a file, restore it in Dropbox before you leave.
  • Wait a week. Use OneDrive for a few days before cancelling, so you notice anything missing while Dropbox still has it.

When you cancel a paid Dropbox plan, your account becomes the free Basic plan with 2 GB. If you have more than that stored, Dropbox stops syncing your files, so it is best to finish moving everything first.
