How to move your files from Dropbox to Google Drive

BY TIM KIM · UPDATED 30 OCTOBER 2026 · 6 MIN READ

Thinking of cancelling Dropbox and keeping everything in Google Drive instead? There are three ways to move your files. The right one depends on how much you have and how much time you want to spend checking it.

IN THIS GUIDE

  1. Before you start: check your Google storage
  2. Way 1: Download from Dropbox, upload to Google Drive
  3. Way 2: Use both desktop apps on your PC
  4. Way 3: Let an app copy and check it for you
  5. Checklist before you cancel Dropbox

Before you start: check your Google storage

A free Google account comes with 15 GB, shared between Google Drive, Gmail and Google Photos. Compare that with how much you use in Dropbox:

  1. In Dropbox, open your account settings and look at Plan to see how much space you use.
  2. Go to one.google.com/storage to see how much Google space you have left.

If Dropbox uses more than you have free in Google, buy more Google storage first, or clear out what you no longer need. Running out halfway is the most common reason a move goes wrong.

Way 1: Download from Dropbox, upload to Google Drive

Best for: a few folders, a few gigabytes. Free, nothing to install.

  1. Sign in at dropbox.com.
  2. Select a folder, then choose Download. Dropbox gives you a .zip file.
  3. On your computer, open the .zip and choose Extract all.
  4. Go to drive.google.com, choose New → Folder upload, and pick the extracted folder.
  5. Repeat for each folder.

Watch out: Dropbox won’t zip very large folders in one go, so big folders have to be downloaded in parts. Your computer also needs enough free space to hold the download and the extracted copy at the same time.

Way 2: Use both desktop apps on your PC

Best for: people comfortable with File Explorer, with enough free disk space for all their files. Free.

  1. Install the Dropbox desktop app and Google Drive for desktop, and sign in to both.
  2. In File Explorer, make sure your Dropbox files are really on your PC. Files marked as online-only are just shortcuts: right-click the Dropbox folder and choose Make available offline, then wait for the download to finish.
  3. Open the Google Drive folder (usually the G: drive) and go into My Drive.
  4. Copy (don’t move) your Dropbox folders into My Drive. Google Drive for desktop uploads them in the background.
  5. Leave the PC on until Google Drive shows that everything has been uploaded.

Watch out: use Copy, not Cut. If you cut files out of the Dropbox folder, Dropbox deletes them from your account too. And if a few files fail to upload, it is easy not to notice among thousands.

Checklist before you cancel Dropbox

Whichever way you choose, go through this list before you stop paying:

  • Compare the totals. Check that the number of files and the total size in Google Drive roughly match Dropbox, folder by folder for the ones that matter most.
  • Open a few files. Pick some old photos and documents at random and make sure they open in Google Drive.
  • Folders shared with you. Folders other people shared with you belong to them. Copy anything you want to keep, because you may lose access later.
  • Links you shared. Links you sent to others point to Dropbox. Share the files again from Google Drive if people still need them.
  • Dropbox-only documents. Dropbox Paper documents may not open in Google Drive. Export them to Word or PDF first.
  • Version history and deleted files. These are not copied. If you need an older version of a file, restore it in Dropbox before you leave.
  • Wait a week. Use Google Drive for a few days before cancelling, so you notice anything missing while Dropbox still has it.

When you cancel a paid Dropbox plan, your account becomes the free Basic plan with 2 GB. If you have more than that stored, Dropbox stops syncing your files, so it is best to finish moving everything first.
