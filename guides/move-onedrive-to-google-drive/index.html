How to move your files from OneDrive to Google Drive

BY TIM KIM · UPDATED 31 OCTOBER 2026 · 6 MIN READ

Living in Google more than Microsoft these days, with Gmail, Google Photos and a Google One plan? Then keeping your files in OneDrive as well may mean paying for storage twice. There are three ways to move them to Google Drive. The right one depends on how much you have and how much time you want to spend checking it.

  2. Way 1: Download from OneDrive, upload to Google Drive

Before you start: check your Google storage and Personal Vault

A free Google account comes with 15 GB, shared between Google Drive, Gmail and Google Photos. Compare that with how much you use in OneDrive:

  1. Go to onedrive.live.com/?v=managestorage to see how much OneDrive space you use.
  2. Go to one.google.com/storage to see how much Google space you have left.

If OneDrive uses more than you have free in Google, buy more Google storage first, or clear out what you no longer need. Running out halfway is the most common reason a move goes wrong.

Personal Vault: files in OneDrive’s Personal Vault are locked behind an extra sign-in, and the vault locks itself again after a few minutes of not being used. It is easy to forget about it. Unlock it, and move anything you want to keep into a normal OneDrive folder before you start.

Way 1: Download from OneDrive, upload to Google Drive

Best for: a few folders, a few gigabytes. Free, nothing to install.

  1. Sign in at onedrive.live.com.
  2. Select a folder, then choose Download. OneDrive gives you a .zip file.
  3. On your computer, open the .zip and choose Extract all.
  4. Go to drive.google.com, choose New → Folder upload, and pick the extracted folder.
  5. Repeat for each folder.

Watch out: OneDrive’s .zip downloads are limited to 10 GB per .zip file, 20 GB in total and 10,000 files (Microsoft Support). Bigger folders have to be downloaded in parts, and it is up to you to keep track of which parts are done. Your computer also needs enough free space to hold the download and the extracted copy at the same time.

Way 2: Use the OneDrive and Google Drive apps on your PC

Best for: people comfortable with File Explorer, with enough free disk space for all their files. Free.

  1. OneDrive is already part of Windows 10 and 11: sign in to it with your Microsoft account. Install Google Drive for desktop and sign in to your Google account.
  2. In File Explorer, make sure your OneDrive files are really on your PC. Files marked as online-only (a cloud icon) are just shortcuts: right-click the OneDrive folder and choose Always keep on this device, then wait for the download to finish.
  3. Open the Google Drive folder (usually the G: drive) and go into My Drive.
  4. Copy (don’t move) your OneDrive folders into My Drive. Google Drive for desktop uploads them in the background.
  5. Leave the PC on until Google Drive shows that everything has been uploaded.

Watch out: use Copy, not Cut. If you cut files out of the OneDrive folder, OneDrive deletes them from your account too. And if a few files fail to upload, it is easy not to notice among thousands. Google Drive also lets each person upload up to 750 GB a day, so a very large OneDrive can take more than one day (Google Help).

Checklist before you change your OneDrive plan

Whichever way you choose, go through this list before you change your plan:

  • Compare the totals. Check that the number of files and the total size in Google Drive roughly match OneDrive, folder by folder for the ones that matter most.
  • Open a few files. Pick some old photos and documents at random and make sure they open in Google Drive.
  • Personal Vault. Check it one last time. Anything still in there has not been copied.
  • Folders shared with you. Folders other people shared with you belong to them. Copy anything you want to keep, because you may lose access later.
  • Links you shared. Links you sent to others point to OneDrive. Share the files again from Google Drive if people still need them.
  • Phone photo backup. If the OneDrive app uploads your phone’s photos, turn on backup in Google Photos instead, so new photos don’t go missing.
  • Version history and deleted files. These are not copied. If you need an older version of a file, restore it in OneDrive before you leave.
  • Wait a week. Use Google Drive for a few days before changing your plan, so you notice anything missing while OneDrive still has it.

Cancel, or just change plan?

Most OneDrive storage comes with a Microsoft 365 subscription, which also includes Word, Excel and Outlook. If you still want those apps, you may only need a smaller plan, not to cancel. Without a subscription you are back to the free 5 GB, shared with your Outlook.com attachments. If you are over that, Microsoft says you can’t upload, edit or sync new files, Outlook.com can stop sending and receiving email, and after six months Microsoft might delete your OneDrive and all files in it (Microsoft Support). So finish moving, then delete what you no longer need from OneDrive, before your plan ends.
